LESSON 07 · WORD FUNDAMENTALS

Paragraph Formatting

Learn how to align paragraphs, control spacing, adjust indentation and organize document content professionally.

1. What is Paragraph Formatting?

Paragraph formatting means changing the arrangement and appearance of a paragraph in a document.

It controls how text is positioned, spaced and displayed on a page.

Remember: Font formatting changes individual characters, while paragraph formatting controls the layout of an entire paragraph.

2. Understanding the Paragraph Group

The Paragraph group is available on the Home tab of Microsoft Word.

It contains several tools, including:

  • Text Alignment
  • Line and Paragraph Spacing
  • Indentation
  • Bullets and Numbering
  • Borders and Shading
  • Show/Hide Formatting Marks

3. Paragraph Alignment

Alignment controls the horizontal position of paragraph text.

Left Alignment

Text starts from the left margin. This is the common default alignment.

This paragraph is left aligned.

Center Alignment

Text is positioned in the centre of the available line width.

This paragraph is center aligned.

Right Alignment

Text is positioned against the right margin.

This paragraph is right aligned.

Justify Alignment

Text is aligned along both the left and right margins by adjusting spacing between words.

This paragraph demonstrates justified alignment. Word adjusts spacing between words so that the text appears aligned with both margins, except where the final line naturally ends.

Alignment Shortcuts

  • Ctrl + L — Left Align
  • Ctrl + E — Center Align
  • Ctrl + R — Right Align
  • Ctrl + J — Justify

4. Line Spacing

Line spacing controls the vertical distance between lines of text in a paragraph.

Common line-spacing options include:

  • 1.0 – Single spacing
  • 1.15 – Slightly increased spacing
  • 1.5 – One-and-a-half spacing
  • 2.0 – Double spacing

Steps to Change Line Spacing

  1. Select the paragraph.
  2. Open the Home tab.
  3. Find the Paragraph group.
  4. Click Line and Paragraph Spacing.
  5. Select the required spacing.
Tip: Many school and college assignments use 1.5 or double line spacing, depending on their formatting guidelines.

5. Paragraph Spacing

Paragraph spacing controls the extra space before or after a paragraph.

Spacing Before

Adds space above a paragraph.

Spacing After

Adds space below a paragraph.

To change these settings, open the Layout tab and look for Spacing Before and After, or open the Paragraph dialog box.

6. Indentation

Indentation controls the distance of a paragraph from the left or right margin.

Left Indent

Moves the entire paragraph inward from the left margin.

Right Indent

Moves the paragraph inward from the right margin.

First Line Indent

Moves only the first line of a paragraph inward.

Hanging Indent

Keeps the first line at the original position while the remaining lines are indented.

Indentation is useful for quotations, references and structured documents.

7. Borders and Shading

Paragraph Borders add lines around or beside a paragraph. Shading adds a background colour behind it.

How to Apply

  1. Select the paragraph.
  2. Go to the Home tab.
  3. Open the Borders dropdown in the Paragraph group.
  4. Choose a border option.
  5. Use Shading to apply a background colour if needed.

These tools help highlight important notices, headings and quotations.

8. Show/Hide Formatting Marks

The Show/Hide button displays non-printing formatting marks such as paragraph marks, spaces and tab characters.

These marks help you understand the structure of a document and identify unwanted spaces or empty paragraphs.

Shortcut: Ctrl + Shift + 8
Formatting marks are visible while editing but do not normally appear in the printed document.

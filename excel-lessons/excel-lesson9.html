Charts and Data Visualization

Turn numbers into meaningful charts and present your data in a clear, professional and visual way.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Understand charts and data visualization.
  • Create charts using Excel data.
  • Use Column, Bar, Line and Pie Charts.
  • Add chart titles, legends and data labels.
  • Change chart types and styles.
  • Format charts for professional reports.

1. What Is Data Visualization?

Data visualization means presenting information using visual elements such as charts, graphs and diagrams.

Charts make it easier to understand comparisons, trends, patterns and proportions.

Example: Instead of reading monthly sales numbers one by one, a chart can help you quickly identify which month had the highest sales.

Why Use Charts?

  • Make large datasets easier to understand.
  • Compare values quickly.
  • Identify trends over time.
  • Present reports professionally.
  • Support better business decisions.

2. Types of Charts in Excel

A. Column Chart

A Column Chart uses vertical bars to compare values across categories.

Best for: Comparing monthly sales, student marks or yearly performance.

Example: Compare sales of January, February, March and April.

B. Bar Chart

A Bar Chart uses horizontal bars to compare different categories.

Best for: Comparing products, departments or employee performance when category names are long.

C. Line Chart

A Line Chart connects data points with lines to show changes over time.

Best for: Monthly sales trends, yearly growth or temperature changes.

D. Pie Chart

A Pie Chart displays how different categories contribute to a whole.

Best for: Expense distribution, market share or budget allocation.

E. Area Chart

An Area Chart displays trends over time with the area below the line filled in.

Best for: Showing changes in totals over time.

F. Scatter Chart

A Scatter Chart displays the relationship between two numerical variables.

Best for: Studying relationships such as study hours and exam marks.

💡 Choose a chart based on the message you want your data to communicate. A chart should make information clearer, not more confusing.

3. How to Create a Chart

Follow these steps to create your first Excel chart:

  1. Enter your data in rows and columns with clear headings.
  2. Select the complete data range, including headings.
  3. Open the Insert tab.
  4. Find the Charts section.
  5. Choose the chart type you need.
  6. Select a chart design or style.
  7. Excel will insert the chart into your worksheet.
Tip: Include category labels and numerical values when selecting your data. This helps Excel create a meaningful chart.

5. Important Chart Elements

  • Chart Title: Describes what the chart represents.
  • Horizontal Axis: Usually displays categories such as months or products.
  • Vertical Axis: Usually displays numerical values.
  • Legend: Explains the different data series.
  • Data Labels: Show values directly on the chart.
  • Gridlines: Help compare values against the chart axes.
  • Plot Area: The area where the data is displayed.
Example Chart Title: Monthly Sales Report – 2026

6. How to Format a Chart

  1. Click the chart to select it.
  2. Open the Chart Design tab.
  3. Choose a chart style or colour combination.
  4. Use Add Chart Element to add titles, labels or gridlines.
  5. Use Format to customize borders, fills and text.
  6. Resize or move the chart as required.

Change Chart Type

  1. Select the chart.
  2. Open Chart Design.
  3. Click Change Chart Type.
  4. Choose another chart type.
  5. Click OK.

7. Move or Resize a Chart

You can move a chart to another location in the worksheet by selecting and dragging it.

To resize a chart, select it and drag one of the corner handles.

You can also use the Move Chart option to place it on a separate chart sheet.

A separate chart sheet is useful when you want to display a chart independently from the worksheet data.

8. Choosing the Right Chart

PurposeRecommended Chart
Compare categoriesColumn or Bar
Show changes over timeLine

Lesson 17: Excel Dashboard & MIS Reporting

SkillSaathi · Excel & Office · Practical Office Skills

1. What is MIS Reporting?

MIS stands for Management Information System. MIS reports organise business data into useful information so managers and teams can monitor performance and make decisions.

Common MIS reports include:

  • Daily sales report
  • Monthly performance report
  • Employee attendance report
  • Inventory or stock report
  • Expense and budget report
  • Customer or order report
Example: A sales manager may use a monthly MIS report to compare sales across departments and identify which team achieved its target.

2. What is an Excel Dashboard?

A dashboard is a visual summary of important information. It uses numbers, charts and tables to show the overall performance of a business or activity.

A useful dashboard should be:

  • Simple and easy to understand
  • Accurate and updated
  • Focused on important information
  • Consistent in formatting
  • Easy to read without unnecessary decoration

4. Create Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

KPIs are important numbers that help measure performance. Common sales dashboard KPIs include:

  • Total Sales
  • Total Target
  • Number of Orders
  • Average Sales
  • Target Achievement Percentage
Example formulas:
Total Sales: =SUM(C2:C6)
Total Target: =SUM(D2:D6)
Average Sales: =AVERAGE(C2:C6)
Number of Records: =COUNTA(A2:A6)
Target Achievement: =IFERROR(SUM(C2:C6)/SUM(D2:D6),0)

Format the target achievement result as Percentage.

5. Department-wise Summary with SUMIF

SUMIF calculates the total sales for a particular department.

=SUMIF(B2:B6,"North",C2:C6)

This formula adds sales values from column C wherever the department in column B is North.

6. Create Charts

Charts make comparisons and trends easier to understand.

  • Column Chart: Compare sales across departments.
  • Bar Chart: Compare categories or teams.
  • Line Chart: Show changes over time.
  • Pie Chart: Show parts of a whole, when there are only a few categories.

Steps to insert a chart:

  1. Select your summary table.
  2. Open the Insert tab.
  3. Choose a suitable chart type.
  4. Add a clear chart title.
  5. Adjust labels and formatting.
Choose a chart based on the question you want to answer. Do not use too many charts on one dashboard.

7. PivotTables for Reporting

A PivotTable summarises large datasets without writing many formulas.

  1. Click any cell inside your data table.
  2. Go to Insert → PivotTable.
  3. Choose where to place the PivotTable.
  4. Drag Department to Rows.
  5. Drag Sales to Values.
  6. Check that the calculation is Sum of Sales.

You can also add Date, Category or Employee fields to analyse the data in different ways.

8. Add Slicers and Make Reports Interactive

Slicers are clickable filters that make PivotTable reports easier to use.

  1. Select a PivotTable.
  2. Open PivotTable Analyze → Insert Slicer.
  3. Select a field such as Department.
  4. Click a slicer button to filter the report.

For date-based reports, use a Timeline if your PivotTable supports it.

After adding new data to the source table, refresh the PivotTable so the report includes the latest records.

9. Build a Simple Dashboard Layout

Arrange your dashboard in a clear order:

  1. Top: Dashboard title and reporting period.
  2. First row: KPI cards for Total Sales, Target and Achievement.
  3. Middle: Department-wise chart and sales trend chart.
  4. Bottom: Summary table and useful notes.
  5. Side or top: Slicers for interactive filtering.

10. Practical Project: Monthly Sales MIS Dashboard

Create a new Excel workbook and complete this project.

Sheet 1 — Raw Data

  • Enter at least 20 sales records.
  • Include Date, Employee, Department, Sales and Target.
  • Convert the data into an Excel Table.

Sheet 2 — Summary

  • Calculate Total Sales and Total Target.
  • Calculate average sales.
  • Prepare department-wise sales using SUMIF or a PivotTable.

Sheet 3 — Dashboard

  • Add KPI values.
  • Add a department comparison chart.
  • Add a sales trend chart.
  • Add a PivotTable slicer if available.
  • Use clear titles and consistent formatting.
Before sharing the report, verify totals, check the reporting dates, refresh PivotTables and review chart labels.

11. Common Dashboard Mistakes

  • Using incorrect or unclean source data.
  • Showing too many charts and colours.
  • Forgetting to refresh PivotTables.
  • Using unclear titles or missing date ranges.
  • Showing percentages without checking the calculation.
  • Sharing confidential employee or customer information without permission.
